Game mechanics: what the challenger touches, and where c2 errs

Questions: does the challenger only look at buys? Are playing actions, their order, buying nothing and playing nothing taken into account? And why has the student stopped improving?

Answers in short.
  • Buys only. The teacher and the student change only buy decisions. Buying nothing (PASS) is always one of the graded candidates. Every other decision is c2's, in the real game and inside every rollout: which action to play, in what order, when to stop playing actions, Throne Room targets, trashing, gains, discards.
  • The rest is the bigger share. Of 246.1 decisions per game with two or more options (both players), buys are 54 (22%). Action plays are 106 (43%), with 3.19 options on average. Gains from Artisan, Remodel, Workshop and Mine (7.4 per game, 10–13 options each) are buy-like choices that the challenger does not touch.
  • c2's buys have no card-level mistakes. Over 430,092 labelled positions, 748 pairs "c2 bought X, the alternative was Y" (at least 300 positions each) were tested. No pair beats c2 on average beyond chance: the best has z = 2.0 among 748 tests. The teacher's wins (55–59%) come from rare, position-specific exceptions. That is why the student, which learns from labels with 16 draws (paired SE about 0.1 per position), has stalled near c2.
  • Action teacher (experiment). 200 games so far: score 56.2% (95% interval 51.5–60.8%) against plain c2. It graded 10187 action decisions and overrode 229 (2.2%): 160 picked a different action, 69 stopped where c2 would play, 0 played where c2 would stop.

4. What this means

  1. The student's plateau is a signal-to-noise problem, not a missing input. The gains are rare (a few % of positions), small, and depend on the position. More positions alone move the regret slowly (0.062 → 0.051 over 340k positions).
  2. The gated teacher already captures them (54.9% vs c2, 59.2% vs c1 on reserved kingdoms). It is the model to use for play now, at about 1 second per buy.
  3. The next gains are likely outside buys: action plays are twice as many decisions, and gains are buy-like. The action teacher measures whether rollouts improve c2 there too. If they do, the same gated teacher can cover both, and gains can be added once the engine has a resume point for gain decisions (an engine change, no rule change).
